Our story

We built GreenProof because
claims aren't proof.

Every "sustainably sourced" label makes a promise. Almost none of them show their work. We set out to close that gap — one traceable supply chain at a time.

Our mission

Turn supply chain complexity into something anyone can verify.

Most sustainability claims today are self-reported, hard to check, and even harder to trust. Brands want to prove their impact. Consumers want to believe them. Auditors are stuck in the middle, chasing spreadsheets. GreenProof exists to remove that friction — by making traceability the default, not a quarterly scramble before an audit.

We think the future of sustainability isn't better marketing copy. It's better data, presented plainly, that holds up under scrutiny — from a certifier, a journalist, or a customer scanning a QR code in a store aisle.

What GreenProof does

One platform, from your supplier notes to your customer's phone.

Structured supply chains

Raw supplier text — emails, spreadsheets, notes — gets parsed into a clean, stage-by-stage map of your product's journey. Raw material, processing, manufacturing, shipping: tracked and editable.

Defensible emissions math

Every carbon figure is calculated from cited factors, not estimated. When someone asks how a number was reached, there's a real answer waiting.

A dashboard that stays honest

Product owners see live progress across every stage of every product — what's verified, what's still incomplete — instead of a static PDF that goes stale the day it's exported.

A label consumers can scan

The same underlying data that satisfies an auditor becomes a simple, scannable label — no separate marketing narrative sitting on top of the real numbers.

What we've learned building this

Traceability breaks down for boring reasons — not because brands don't care.

01

Most sustainability data doesn't live in a clean database — it lives in emails, PDFs, and someone's memory. Any tool that assumes tidy structured input from day one will go unused.

02

Auditors and consumers are asking the same underlying question — "can you prove this?" — just at different levels of detail. One data model can serve both if it's designed honestly from the start.

03

Supply chains change constantly — a new supplier, a swapped material, a shipping route. A traceability tool that requires a rebuild every time something changes will always be a step behind reality.
